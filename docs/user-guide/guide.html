This guide describes Shorashim 1.0.0: how to install the app, find your way around, and use each part of the course. The app helps you study; it does not replace a teacher.

1About the app

Shorashim (Hebrew for “roots”) is an offline Windows app for studying the foundations of Judaism and for calm, step-by-step preparation for giyur and the beit din interview.

The course is written in the language of Torah and Tanakh — the Written and Oral Torah. It is consistently pro-Israel: Eretz Israel is the heritage the Torah gives the Jewish people, Jerusalem is the eternal undivided capital, and the State of Israel is the Jewish people’s return home.

The app is meant to be used every day in short sittings: a lesson of about five to ten minutes, a few flashcards, a step on the Ma’alot ladder, or a handful of interview questions. Nothing here replaces a teacher. The materials need review by a rabbi of your community; the app helps you study.

2Install and first launch

Shorashim runs on Windows 10 or Windows 11. You do not need administrator rights. The only file you need is the installer, Shorashim-Setup.exe.

  1. Open the latest release: github.com/Mitroshenkov87/shorashim/releases/latest.
  2. Download Shorashim-Setup.exe.
  3. Run the installer. It unpacks the program, the content folder (lessons, cards, questions, prayers and the calendar) and the music, creates shortcuts, and starts Shorashim.

Progress is stored separately, in %APPDATA%\Shorashim, and is left as it is if you install the app again. Everything works offline: once the installer has finished, you do not need a network connection to study.

The first time the app opens, you will see the welcome screen. Read the short note about rabbi review, then press Start. You will be asked to choose a movement — Orthodox, Conservative (Masorti), or Reform. Each movement is presented on its own terms, with equal care, and each keeps its own progress. You can change this later in Settings, or with the level switch at the top of the home screen.

3Home and the sidebar

After the welcome screen, Shorashim opens on Lessons — the home view. The left sidebar is the main way around the app. The main pane shows today’s study at a glance.

The daily strip

At the top you will see today’s XP toward the daily goal (20 XP), how many days in a row you have met the goal, and how many flashcards are due today. One lesson marked as read gives 10 XP. A hint under the strip tells you how much is still needed for the day.

The sidebar

  • Lessons — the eight sections of the course, and this home view.
  • Calendar — the Jewish date, the day’s meaning, the weekly Torah portion, and candle and fast times.
  • The Land — a map of Eretz Israel. The default view, Israel today, follows official Israeli maps. Separate layers show the bounds of the Land in the Torah and the tribal allotments. This guide does not walk through the map; open it from the sidebar or from the card on the home screen when you want it.
  • Prayers — prayers and blessings for each day, with Hebrew, a translation and a transliteration.
  • Flashcards — spaced repetition of what you have already read.
  • Quiz — Ma’alot, the fifteen-step ladder of knowledge.
  • Tests — a knowledge check for each section, and a general exam.
  • Progress — your level, statistics, and the collection of objects you have unlocked.
  • Settings — movement, theme, font, reviewer mode, and other options.
  • Sources — a reading list for going further.

Below the daily strip, the home screen offers a Climb button for Ma’alot, a Prepare button for the beit din interview trainer, and the Item of the Day — a 3D object, such as a Torah scroll, that you can turn with the mouse.

4Lessons and study levels

The course has eight sections, from the foundations of faith to the beit din. Lessons are short, about five to ten minutes each:

  1. Foundations
  2. Hebrew and Prayer
  3. Shabbat
  4. Kashrut
  5. Festivals
  6. The life cycle and the family
  7. Ethics
  8. Beit din

Open a section, then open a lesson. The text is written at the movement you chose: Orthodox, Conservative (Masorti), or Reform. Where the movements differ, the lesson says so plainly, and each is described on its own terms.

After the lesson text you will usually find:

  • Main points — a short recap of the lesson, for review.
  • What the beit din asks — typical questions on this topic, with a model answer you can reveal after you have tried in your own words.
  • Mark as read — this counts toward your progress and the daily goal.
  • Self-check — a short set of closed questions on the lesson.
  • To this lesson’s flashcards — jump straight to the cards for this lesson.

A section also has two modes, Read and Prepare. Read is for understanding the material. Prepare gathers the work that gets you ready for a test: the section’s flashcards, a rehearsal of beit-din questions, and the section test.

Sections ordinarily open in order. To unlock the next one, read the lessons and pass the section test (the pass mark is 80%). If you would rather move freely, turn on Open every section in Settings.

5Flashcards

Every lesson already has a deck of cards. You do not create them yourself. Open them from the lesson, from the section, or from Flashcards in the sidebar.

The first time you open a deck, a short “How does it work?” panel explains the method. Then you study as follows:

  1. Read the question, recall the answer in your mind, and click the card to flip it.
  2. Grade yourself honestly: Recalled or Didn’t recall.
  3. Recalled — the card comes back in 1, then 3, 7, 14, and 30 days.
  4. Didn’t recall — the card is shown again in this sitting, and the schedule starts over at day 1.
  5. A card counts as learned when you recall it at the 30-day interval.

Where it helps, the back of a card also notes how the same point is taught in the other movements. Cards due today are counted on the home screen, so a short daily review is easy to keep up.

6Quiz and Ma’alot

Ma’alot (Hebrew מעלות, “steps”) is a fifteen-step ladder of knowledge, from easier questions to harder ones. Open it from Quiz in the sidebar, or press Climb on the home screen.

The ladder follows the saying in Pirkei Avot 5:21: “At five years — to Mikra, at ten — to Mishnah, at thirteen — to mitzvot, … at fifteen — to Gemara.” Steps 1–5 are easy, 6–10 medium, and 11–15 hard. Questions are taken from the sections you have already opened, at your study level.

Steps 5 (“Mikra”) and 10 (“Mishnah”) are safe steps. After a mistake you return to the last safe step you have already passed, marked with a gold edge on the ladder. You may stop at any moment; the steps you have already passed stay with you. Your best climb is kept as a record.

You have three hints, each once per climb:

  • 50/50 — two wrong answers are removed.
  • Lesson hint — a pointer back to the lesson that teaches this point.
  • Ask the rabbi — a steer toward the source, not the letter of the answer.

A timer is optional: no timer, 30 seconds, or 20 seconds for each question. If the time runs out, the answer is counted as wrong. You can set this on the Ma’alot start screen or in Settings. Answers can also be chosen from the keyboard (A–D or 1–4).

7Tests

Open Tests in the sidebar for a knowledge check. There is a test for each section, and a General exam over the course as a whole. The pass mark is 80%. A passed section test, together with the lessons you have read, is what opens the next section.

Questions may be true or false, multiple choice, or another closed form. When you finish, you can Review your mistakes. If you press Leave in the middle of a test, the app asks you to confirm. That attempt will not be counted, and you may come back whenever you like.

8Beit din interview trainer

The beit din does not quiz you from a textbook. The judges want to understand who you are, why you have come, and how you already live. The trainer collects questions rabbis often ask at the interview, with explanations of what lies behind them. These are not ready-made answers to memorize. The judges can spot a memorized text right away; answer in your own words and about your real life. If you do not know something, or do not yet observe something, say so honestly.

There are 68 questions, in three modes:

  • Topics — read the questions and the model answers at your own pace, grouped by theme (motivation, faith and the oneness of G-d, Shabbat, kashrut, festivals, family and community, mitzvot and halacha, prayer and Hebrew, and a knowledge check).
  • Practice — choose the right answer from several options. The result is checked at once and counts toward your review schedule.
  • Rehearsal — a run of ten questions, closer to the interview itself. You may also answer aloud.

The counters at the top — practiced, ready, and rehearsals — show how far you have come. From the home screen, Prepare opens this trainer directly.

9Jewish calendar

Calendar in the sidebar opens the Jewish year. The Today tab is the usual view; One month shows a month at a time; Settings is where you set the place used for candle and fast times.

For each day the app shows:

  • The civil date and the Jewish date, including the Hebrew form of the date.
  • About this day — the meaning of the day, and whether a fast falls on it.
  • Candle and fast times — candle lighting, havdalah, and when fasts begin and end. An approximate place is needed (a city or coordinates). Set it once on the Calendar’s own Settings tab; the times then appear automatically.
  • Weekly portion — the Torah reading for the coming Shabbat.

10Prayers

Prayers gathers prayers and blessings for each day — for example the Shema Yisrael, the central declaration of faith. Open a prayer from the list. Each one can be shown in three ways, together or one at a time:

  • Hebrew — the text of the prayer.
  • Translation — so you can follow the meaning.
  • Transliteration — a pronunciation guide under the Hebrew. You can hide or show this in Settings (Show transliteration).

A short note on the prayer tells you when it is said and how. You can change the text size on the prayer itself (the Hebrew scales too), save a prayer you return to often, and go back to the full list with All prayers.

If you do not yet read Hebrew, pray with the translation and the transliteration beside it, and grow into the Hebrew in your own time. The app is a help for practice; the custom of your community is what you should follow.

11Progress

Open Progress in the sidebar for a picture of your study: the daily goal, your streak, total XP, and how ready you are in each part of the course.

XP is given for the work itself: 10 for a lesson marked as read, 1 for each graded flashcard (a “Didn’t recall” still counts, because the work was done), 5 for a finished deck, 20 for a passed section test (plus 10 for a perfect score), 2 for each correct answer on Ma’alot, 15 for a practice beit din, and 1 for each correct answer in a self-check, a rehearsal, or a review of mistakes.

The streak is how many days in a row you have met the daily goal. If today’s goal is not yet met, the streak is safe until the end of the day. Every seven days of a streak earns a streak freeze (you can hold up to two). If you miss a day, a freeze is used automatically and the streak survives.

The same screen also shows readiness for the interview trainer, and the Item collection. Each object is added to the shelf when you read the lesson that mentions it — a Torah scroll, a tallit, Shabbat candles, and others. Open an object to turn it with the mouse and read a short note about it. Here the Second Temple is open. The Item of the Day on the home screen is drawn from this collection.

Each movement keeps its own progress. If you switch from Orthodox to Reform, or the other way, you will see the path you have already walked in that movement, not a shared score mixed together.

12Settings

Settings is where you fit the app to how you study. The options below are the ones you will use most.

  • Movement — Orthodox, Conservative (Masorti), or Reform. Each movement keeps its own progress. Use Change movement when you want to switch.
  • Theme — Dark (the default) or Leather.
  • Font size — from A− to A++. Hebrew scales too.
  • Open reviewer mode — unrestricted mode for a rabbi or a tester: any section and any part of the app can be opened. Progress is not counted, and your real progress is left unchanged when you turn the mode off.
  • Open every section — study freely, without unlocking sections in order.
  • Show transliteration — a pronunciation guide under the Hebrew in prayers.
  • Quote of the Day — a panel on the right of a wide window: a quote and today’s date.
  • 3D models — live objects you can turn with the mouse (a graphics card is required).
  • Ma’alot — a time limit for each question on the ladder: no timer, 30 seconds, or 20 seconds.
  • Music — quiet instrumental melodies, off by default. Turn them on here if you want them.

Settings also lets you reset your progress. That cannot be undone, except by importing a file you saved earlier, so use it only when you mean to start again. Theme, font and music are kept.

13Rabbi review

All course content must be checked by a rabbi. A lesson, a card, a question or a prayer may still show the badge Awaiting a rabbi’s review until that check is complete. The checklists, with a stable id for every item (lessons, cards, questions, interview items, prayers, calendar days and map notes), are in the project documentation:

  • docs/RABBI-REVIEW.md
  • docs/RABBI-REVIEW.en.md

The two files share the same item ids. If you are a rabbi or a knowledgeable reader and would like to help, please open an issue and cite the item id.

A note on the app’s place

The app helps you study; it does not replace a teacher. These materials need review by a rabbi of your community. Follow the custom of the beit din that is walking with you.

May your study be fruitful, and may it bring you closer to Torah, to the Jewish people, and to Eretz Israel.
